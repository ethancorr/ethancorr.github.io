Research

Convex optimization at the foundation of neural network training.

I work on the mathematical theory underlying how neural networks learn, with a particular focus on convex optimization, sparsity, and the structure of the solutions these models find.

Focus areas

What I work on

Convex Optimization for Neural Networks

Reformulating neural network training problems in convex terms to better understand what solutions these networks actually find, and why.

Sparse & Measure-Theoretic Optimization

Drawing on sparse optimization over measures to characterize and compute solutions as regularization is varied.

Gaussian Process Modeling

Probabilistic modeling methods for characterizing physical hazards, such as volcanic debris flow.

Current work

PhD research

Advised by Dr. Gregory Ongie, Department of Mathematical and Statistical Sciences, Marquette University.

My dissertation, "An Analytical Approach to Neural Net Behavior," develops theory connecting classical sparse-optimization techniques to the training of shallow neural networks, with the goal of describing how optimal solutions behave as a key regularization parameter changes. The broader aim is to bring the tools of convex analysis to bear on questions about neural network behavior that are usually approached only empirically, building a more principled understanding of what these models learn and why. This work builds on my MS essay, "Characterization of 1D Interpolating Neural Networks" (2025), and has been supported by two Northwestern Mutual Data Science Institute (NMDSI) Student Scholars grants (Summer and Fall 2026).
