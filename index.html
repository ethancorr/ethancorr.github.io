About

A mathematician who also ships production data science.

I split my time between a PhD in mathematics and a full-time data science role in industry, and I think that combination is a strength, not a compromise.

I'm a PhD candidate in the Department of Mathematical and Statistical Sciences at Marquette University, advised by Dr. Gregory Ongie. My research sits at the intersection of convex optimization and neural network theory, asking why and how these models learn the functions they do, and building tools to describe that behavior mathematically.

Alongside my PhD, I work full-time at Clarios in Glendale, WI, where I've spent the last several years moving from mechanical design engineering into AI/ML algorithm development and now data science, currently building supply chain optimization models and forecasting tools for the business. Before graduate school, I completed a BS in Mechanical Engineering and Mathematics at Marquette, with undergraduate research applying Gaussian process modeling to volcanic debris flow.

I'm also involved in Milwaukee's data science and STEM community, including mentoring undergraduate interns, leading K-12 STEM outreach events, and occasional talks at local conferences and company tech events on data science, engineering, and applied mathematics, and I hope to get even more involved going forward.
